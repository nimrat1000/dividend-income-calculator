Dividend Calculator Hub: Which Tool Should You Use?

Not every dividend question needs the same calculator. Start with the answer you actually want.

Alex: “I searched for a dividend calculator and found five different tools. Which one do I need?”
Me: “Tell me the question first. Then we can pick the math.”

1. “How much will I receive each month?”

Use a monthly dividend income calculator. If you know the number of units and distribution per unit, multiply them. If you only know investment amount and an assumed annual yield, estimate the annual cash and divide by 12. Remember that monthly average and monthly payment schedule aren't always the same.

Open monthly dividend income calculator

2. “What yield am I actually getting?”

Use a dividend yield calculator to compare an annualized payment rate with an investment price or amount. A high displayed yield is not a guarantee of a high total return. The method used to annualize distributions matters.

Open monthly dividend yield calculator

3. “How much do I need to invest for C$1,000 or US$1,000 a month?”

Use a required-capital estimate. At an assumed 5% annual cash yield, a C$12,000 yearly income target would require C$240,000. At an assumed 10%, it would require C$120,000. These are arithmetic scenarios, not forecasts. The same math works in US dollars if you keep all inputs in US dollars.

Required capital = annual income goal ÷ assumed annual yield

See the $1,000/month investment guide

4. “Should I spend the payments or reinvest them?”

A reinvestment comparison helps you explore how many units you could accumulate under an assumed price and payout path. It should not imply that distributions or prices remain fixed. Use it to compare scenarios, not predict outcomes.

Read dividend reinvestment vs taking cash and the ETF income decision guide.

5. “What happens after taxes?”

Taxes depend on account type, jurisdiction, income type and individual circumstances. In Canada, dividend tax treatment differs between eligible and non-eligible dividends and may not match the treatment of all ETF distributions. Cross-border withholding can also matter. A calculator can organize an estimate, but it cannot replace your tax records or professional guidance.

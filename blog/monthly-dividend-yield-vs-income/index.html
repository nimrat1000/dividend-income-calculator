Monthly Dividend Yield vs Monthly Dividend Income: What Is the Difference?

Two numbers sit next to each other on your investing screen. One is a percentage. The other pays your bills. They are not interchangeable.

Alex: “One ETF shows 8% yield and another shows C$60 a month. Which is better?”
Me: “First, we need to compare the same thing.”

Yield is a rate. Income is an amount.

Distribution yield describes a payment relative to an investment value or share price, depending on the provider's method. Monthly dividend income is the amount of cash paid to you. An 8% annualized distribution yield does not mean an ETF sends 8% of your account balance each month.

Let's use the same C$12,000 investment

Assume ETF A has an annual distribution yield of 5% and ETF B has 10%, both unchanged for a full year. Ignoring taxes, costs and currency conversion:

Assumed annual yieldAnnual cashMonthly average
5%C$600C$50
10%C$1,200C$100

ETF B offers a larger hypothetical cash payout, but that alone doesn't make it a better investment. The value of the fund can rise or fall, and higher payouts may come with different risks or strategies.

Why does a yield percentage jump when the ETF price falls?

Imagine a fund pays C$1.20 per unit annually. At a C$20 unit price, that equals 6%. If the price falls to C$15 while the payment stays C$1.20, the indicated yield becomes 8%. The cash did not increase. The price used in the calculation fell.

C$1.20 ÷ C$20 = 6%
C$1.20 ÷ C$15 = 8%

Don't confuse distributions with total return

Suppose Alex starts with C$12,000, collects C$1,200, and ends with ETF units worth C$10,000. His total value is C$11,200 before taxes and costs. that's a C$800 loss despite receiving C$1,200 cash. Yield answers a cash-flow question; total return addresses the combined outcome.

Alex: “So I should check both the payout and what happened to my balance?”
Me: “Every time. That's the difference between planning income and understanding performance.”

Which calculator should I use?

Use the monthly dividend yield calculator when you want to compare payout rates. Use the monthly dividend income calculator when you want an estimated cash amount. For the bigger picture, read Spend or Reinvest ETF Distributions?.

Frequently asked questions

Does a high distribution yield guarantee high returns?

No. A fund can pay substantial cash while losing market value.

Why can two sites show different yields for the same ETF?

They may use different price dates, payment periods or yield definitions. Always check the methodology and date.

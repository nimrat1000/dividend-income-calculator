How to Calculate Your Monthly Dividend Payout

You know how many shares you own. You see a distribution amount. But how much cash should actually arrive?

Alex: “My ETF says it pays 20 cents per unit. I own 150 units. Does that mean I make 20 cents?”
Me: “Not quite. The payment is per unit, so the math gets more interesting.”

Start with the payment per unit

Suppose Alex owns 150 ETF units and the fund announces a C$0.20 monthly distribution per unit. Multiply the two numbers:

150 units × C$0.20 = C$30 per month

If the fund continues paying that amount for 12 months, Alex would receive C$360 in a year before taxes and costs. The important phrase is if the fund continues. Distributions are not guaranteed.

What if the ETF pays quarterly?

Imagine a different ETF pays C$0.60 per unit every quarter. Alex still owns 150 units. Each quarterly payment is C$90. For budgeting, C$90 ÷ 3 gives a C$30 monthly average. It does not mean the ETF sends C$30 every month. Payment frequency matters when bills have deadlines.

Can I calculate income using dividend yield?

Yes, for a rough estimate. Say Alex has C$10,000 invested and assumes a 6% annual cash distribution yield. The estimate is C$10,000 × 0.06 = C$600 per year, or C$50 a month on average. But a quoted yield can be historical, trailing or annualized from the latest distribution. Check which definition the fund uses.

Investment × assumed annual yield ÷ 12 = estimated monthly cash

What changes the actual payout?

Four things can change the result: the number of units owned, the distribution per unit, the fund's payment schedule and taxes or currency conversion. Reinvesting a payment may buy more units, but the number purchased depends on the market price at that time.

Alex: “So C$30 is income, but it doesn't automatically mean C$30 profit?”
Me: “Exactly. If the value of your units falls, the overall investment return may be lower or even negative.”

Frequently asked questions

Does a 12% yield mean I receive 1% every month?

Only as a simple annualized average if the distribution remains unchanged. The payment schedule and the yield methodology can produce different actual cash flows.

Are ETF distributions guaranteed?

No. Funds can increase, decrease or suspend distributions, and their share prices can move independently of cash payments.

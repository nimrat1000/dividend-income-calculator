Same annual income, different payment schedules

Assume $1,200 of annual distributions, split into equal payments. The monthly average is $100 in every row. This is a hypothetical comparison, before taxes and fees.

The weekly figure is rounded; actual calendars and issuer schedules vary. Twice monthly means 24 payments in a typical year; every two weeks usually means 26. Always use the issuer’s published schedule.

Payment frequency does not determine annual yield

Splitting the same annual cash amount into more payments does not increase it. In a reinvestment model, timing can affect compounding, but that is a separate assumption from the cash-income calculation.

Example: quarterly income and a monthly bill

Suppose you receive $300 in March and have a $100 bill each month. The March receipt could be allocated across three upcoming bills, but it does not fund bills due before the payment arrives unless you already have cash available. Record the actual receipt date and each expense date.

Build a useful payment calendar

  1. Record the ticker, currency, expected payment date and estimated amount.
  2. Mark the payment received only after it arrives; record the actual amount.
  3. Separate reinvested distributions from cash kept for spending.
  4. Compare available cash with upcoming bills, rather than relying only on an annual average.

Amounts can vary between payments. A recent payment multiplied by 52 or 12 is an annualized estimate, not a promise of future income.
